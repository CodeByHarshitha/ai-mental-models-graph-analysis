# Summary of Findings: Human Perceptions of AI as Graph Networks

## 1. Dataset & Network Overview
- **Valid Participants Analyzed:** 30 (Synthetic dataset explicitly labeled `is_synthetic=True`)
- **Collective Graph Size:** 12 canonical concepts, 55 association edges, Network Density = 0.8333
- **Connected Components:** 1 (all concepts form a single connected conceptual ecosystem)

## 2. Answers to Research Questions
1. **Most Strongly Associated Concepts with AI:** The top concepts by weighted degree centrality are **AI, Data, Humans, Automation, Algorithms**.
2. **Similarity Across Mental Models:** Pairwise participant similarity averages **0.374** (range: **0.174** to **0.974**), showing shared core vocabulary around *AI* and *Data* alongside sharp structural divergence across socio-technical sub-networks.
3. **Central Bridge Concepts:** **Data** exhibits the highest weighted betweenness centrality, acting as the primary conceptual bridge linking technical mechanisms (*Algorithms*, *Learning*) to societal outcomes (*Jobs*, *Bias*, *Creativity*).
4. **Participant Grouping:** Agglomerative clustering identified **3 distinct perception clusters** with a silhouette score of **0.2497**:
- **Cluster 1: Creativity + Humans + Data** (8 participants — e.g., Graphic Designer, Content Writer, Marketing Lead, Musician)
- **Cluster 2: Data + Automation + Algorithms** (14 participants — e.g., Software Engineer, Data Analyst, CS Student, IT Administrator)
- **Cluster 3: Bias + Humans + Jobs** (8 participants — e.g., Policy Researcher, Journalist, Labor Union Rep, Law Student)
5. **Dominant Cognitive Patterns:** People do not view AI monolithically; instead, mental models organize around four recurring frames: (a) technical systems & automation, (b) algorithmic bias & labor impact, (c) human-AI creative augmentation, and (d) pragmatic everyday decision support.
